## New characters for every story

Every reel gets its own new characters: new names, faces, ages, places and voices, chosen to fit that kural's relationship. Nobody carries over from one reel to the next, so each reel feels like a fresh story and viewers aged 25–34 see a different slice of their own life every time.

**How each story's cast is built**

- **2 or 3 characters per story**, mostly adults aged 22–35, with a child when the story needs one (a son or daughter, a younger sibling, a niece). When a child is on screen, the K0301 rule stays: no adult shouting at, pointing at or towering over the child, because Flow blocks that. The child's reaction tells it instead.
- **The script writes a small character sheet first:** name, age, job, look, outfit, and how they talk to each other. Every image and clip prompt is built from it, so each character stays the same within that reel.
- **New reference images per story**, made in Flow before any clip. Images are free in Flow, so this costs no credits, only about 10 minutes of your time to approve the faces.
- **New voices per story**, matched to each character's gender and age: a male voice, a female voice, or a lighter, younger voice for a child. Flow's own voice is used when the Tamil is clear and the character sounds the same in every clip. Otherwise our Gemini voice is fitted onto Flow's lip movement, as on K0301. You hear both at review. The pipeline keeps a list of names, looks and voices already used, so no face, name or voice repeats too soon.
- **Places change with the story:** a flat in Velachery, an office on OMR, a college canteen, a wedding hall, Besant Nagar beach, a bus to Madurai.

**One thing stays the same in every reel:** the channel narrator (the Charon voice from K0301) says the kural over the lesson card. With a new cast every time, that voice, the badge and the "குறள் N" title are what make it feel like one series. Inside the story, the wiser person in that episode asks a question instead of lecturing, the way Thatha did.

**The trade-off:** viewers can't follow a favourite character, and every reel needs new faces approved, so the first clip of each reel may need one more redo. The credit budget below allows for that.

**Thatha and Kavin** don't have to disappear. The two can return for a monthly kids' special that parents save and share. Thatha can also appear now and then on a video call inside a new story, as that family's grandfather asking his one question. This is the only exception to new characters every time, and it reuses his K0301 reference images, so it costs nothing extra.

## Step by step

Setup takes about two days and costs no video credits; after that, each reel is roughly one day of Flow credits, made the day before or two days before it posts.

**One-time setup (after you approve)**

1. **Update the pipeline** on your Mac, in one change you can review (table below).
2. **Make the new reference images** in Flow: the characters and places for the first two stories. Images are free in Flow, so this costs no credits. You approve each face before it's used.
3. **Voices are picked automatically, with no voice tests.** The script gives each character a voice from our voice table to match their gender, age and personality, and marks each line with its emotion (calm, angry, teasing, tearful). No test requests are used: the only voice requests are the reel's final lines.
4. **Write the first four scripts** (788, 1330, 100, 467) in one session, and you approve them.

**Every week**

1. **Every day:** make the next story's character and place images (free, you approve the faces), then its clips with that day's 50 credits, so no credits go to waste. Continuity review before anything is marked done.
2. **Day before posting:** render, then you watch the final cut and approve it.
3. **Posting day:** I fill in the YouTube and Instagram uploads (title, description, tags, caption, cover as thumbnail, AI label on, not made for kids) and schedule them for the best slot (see "Posting time" below). You make the file picks, as with K0301.
4. **Same evening:** you share the reel to your Story with a poll and reply to comments in the first hour.
5. **Sunday:** the weekly report ranks the week's reels on 3 s hold, average watch, saves, shares and comments, and updates the lessons file the scripts read.

**Credit budget per reel: about 70.** Seven clips (four 8 s clips and three 4 s clips) cost 69 credits. One redo is allowed per reel; a second redo needs your OK. Three reels a week need about 210–255 credits, and your account gets about 400 a week (50 a day plus the 200 monthly), so there is room for redos and a spare day. The script will also plan shots that reuse one clip for two cuts, which K0301 already does.

## How the Flow prompts avoid redos

Redos can be cut to almost none, but not to zero: Flow gives a slightly different result every time, even with the same prompt. The plan is to catch every problem at the image stage, where redos are free, so the paid clip step starts from a picture that's already right.

**Each shot is made in two steps**

1. **Start image (free).** The prompt is built automatically from the script and attaches up to 3 reference images (the characters first, then the place). We check it against the story sheet and redo it for free until it passes.
2. **Clip (paid).** Made only from an approved start image. The prompt asks for one simple movement and tells Flow to change nothing else.

**What a start-image prompt contains, in this order**

| Part | Example for K0788, shot 1 |
| --- | --- |
| Format and style | Vertical 9:16 frame. Warm, painterly 3D animation look, soft light, gentle film grain |
| Place and light | Vignesh's bedroom in a small Chennai flat, 3 am, only the blue light of his phone, a ceiling fan |
| Each character, look and outfit | Vignesh, 29: thin, short black hair, thin moustache. Wearing (same in every shot): grey vest and a checked lungi |
| Props | His phone: black, cracked screen protector, glowing |
| The shot | Vignesh half sitting up in bed, squinting at the ringing phone. Medium close-up from the side of the bed |
| Reference images | "Image 1 is Vignesh, image 2 is the bedroom. Match face, hair, clothes and place exactly; don't copy the layout" |
| Always added | No text, letters, logos or subtitles anywhere |

**What a clip prompt contains**

- One action only: "Vignesh sits up fast and presses the phone to his ear."
- The camera: "Camera stays still" (we never zoom or pan).
- Who talks and when: "From 1 s to 4 s Vignesh is talking; nobody else is on screen". This keeps lip sync right.
- A lock line: "Keep his face, hair, clothes, the room, the light and every object exactly as in the start frame; nothing appears, disappears or changes colour. No on-screen text."

**The K0301 redos, and the rule that now prevents each one**

| What went wrong on K0301 | Rule from now on |
| --- | --- |
| Kavin's backpack came back in a shot | Props live only in the story sheet, never in a character's outfit, and each shot lists exactly which props are visible |
| The lamp was lit too early, 3 tries | The light and the time of day are fixed per scene, with the shot where they change |
| Clip blocked: an adult's hand jabbing at a child | No adult shouting at, pointing at or towering over a child; the child's reaction carries it |
| Style drifted to realistic, face changed, a name tag appeared | The style line and the reference images go into every prompt, "no text" is always added, and the image passes review before the clip |
| Flow's picker couldn't find older images, so they were uploaded again | The pipeline searches the Flow project by name before generating anything again |
| A failed clip sat unnoticed | Clips are checked every 30–60 s, with a 4 minute limit and up to 2 automatic retries |

Short, plain sentences work best in Flow: one action per clip, no metaphors, and every detail that must stay the same written out. A clip of 4–8 s with one movement fails far less often than a long clip with several things happening.

## Flow credits and extra Gmail accounts

**Short answer:** yes, a character can be reused on another account, but the results won't be identical, and splitting the work across accounts to get more free credits puts your main account at risk. I recommend staying on one account and spending credits better.

**Can the character be exported?** Flow has no export button for a character, but it doesn't need one. A character in our pipeline is just its reference images (saved in `assets/characters`) plus the written description in the character sheet. Upload the same images to any Google account's Flow and the same model uses them the same way.

**Will the video be the same?** About as consistent as on your own account, not exactly the same. Flow gives a different result every time, even on one account, which is why K0301 needed continuity redos. Consistency comes from the reference images, the bible and the review, not from which account runs it. Two practical catches: each account needs its own Flow project with the references uploaded again, and a free account [may not make videos at peak hours](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/16526234?hl=en) (about 7:30–10:30 pm IST).

**The risk.** Google's [Flow credits page](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/16526234?hl=en) and [Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16625148?hl=en) don't explicitly ban having several accounts. Still, running one channel's production across several accounts to get past the daily limit is exactly the pattern abuse checks look for. Flow already showed an "unusual activity" warning on K0301 during a burst of failed clips. If Google restricts accounts, the one you lose could be your AI Plus account with all the projects and references. So I won't automate across several accounts.

**Better ways to get more out of credits**

1. **Generate every day.** Daily credits don't roll over, so a day without generating is 50 credits lost. With this, 3 reels a week fit easily (previous section).
2. **Fewer, smarter clips.** 7 clips per reel instead of 10, with one 8 s clip cut into two shots where the story allows.
3. **Fewer redos.** Most K0301 waste was redos (lamp, backpack, style drift) and failed uploads. Approving each story's faces before any clip is made, plus the pre-checks, cuts these.
4. **Buy credits when needed.** AI Plus lets you buy extra AI credits, for a busy week or a festival special.
5. **Upgrade later.** Google AI Pro gives 1,000 monthly credits instead of 200. Worth it once the channel earns, or if you want 5–7 reels a week.
6. **Family sharing.** Google says AI credits can be shared with your Google One family group. Worth checking if family members have plans, since it's the official way to pool credits.
